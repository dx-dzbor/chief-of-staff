# Fictional onboarding conversation

This example uses invented people and projects. It shows the pace of `/onboard`, not facts to copy into a real owner's files.

**Agent:** I'll learn what you own, what matters now, and your next move, then make a first brief. What do you do at work, and who depends on you? A rough dump is fine.

**Owner:** I'm Maya, an operations lead at the fictional Northstar Labs. I help the support and product teams launch changes without surprising customers.

**Agent:** What one outcome matters most in the next two weeks? What would done look like, and is there a date?

**Owner:** Get the Aurora pilot ready for one support team by 16 October. Done means the runbook and escalation path are agreed.

**Agent:** What's blocking it, and what's the next concrete action? Who owns that action?

**Owner:** The escalation owner is unclear. I'll ask Sam to name one at tomorrow's review.

**Agent:** Here's the operating picture I'd record: You coordinate support and product launches. Your current outcome is an Aurora pilot ready for one support team by 16 October, with an agreed runbook and escalation path. You own asking Sam to name the escalation owner at tomorrow's review. I don't yet know the review time or Sam's role. I propose updating `me/index.md`, `me/current-drive.md`, and `me/weekly-goals.md` with those facts. Correct anything before I write them.

**Owner:** That's right.

**Agent:** Saved the three files and made today's brief. Top action: ask Sam to name Aurora's escalation owner at tomorrow's review. The brief is in `briefs/YYYY-MM-DD.html`. Would a weekday morning brief help, or would another recurring check be more useful?

**Owner:** I'll run it manually for now.

**Agent:** Great. Run `/brief` whenever you want the next one. After work, paste rough notes into `/debrief`; the system can learn the rest as you go.
